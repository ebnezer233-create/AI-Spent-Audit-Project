# Economics

Assumptions are rough but useful. Suppose Credex earns value when a startup buys discounted AI credits through them. If a high-intent lead has $2,000/month in AI infrastructure spend and Credex can capture 10% gross margin on a discounted-credit transaction, annual spend is $24,000 and gross profit is about $2,400. If the relationship repeats or expands, a converted lead could reasonably be worth $2,000–$5,000 in gross profit.

Channel CAC from the GTM plan is mostly time cost. Direct founder outreach: if 100 messages take 8 hours and produce 10 audits, 3 calls, and 1 purchase, at a $50/hour internal cost CAC is $400. Reddit/HN/X teardown posts: if a post takes 2 hours and generates 30 audits, 8 emails, 2 calls, and 0.5 purchases on average, CAC is about $200–$300 per customer. Partner posts with founder communities could be lower if Credex already has credibility.

Profitability funnel target: audit completed to email capture = 30%; email capture to consultation = 20% for high-savings users; consultation to purchase = 25%. From 1,000 audits, assume 30% are high-savings = 300. 90 emails, 18 consultations, 4–5 purchases. At $2,400 gross profit each, that is $9,600–$12,000 gross profit before support costs.

To drive $1M ARR in 18 months, the tool would need either high volume or larger average buyers. For example, 4,000 audits/month with 1% becoming customers = 40 customers/month. If average annualized gross profit is $2,500, that is $100,000 annualized gross profit added per month. Over 10–12 strong months, $1M ARR/gross-profit run rate becomes possible. This requires pricing pain to be widespread, Credex inventory to match the tools users actually buy, and the audit to produce trust rather than generic savings claims.
